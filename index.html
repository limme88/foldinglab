Mission

Research shouldn't depend on who you already know.

Many high school research opportunities go to students whose families already have connections to labs and universities. Folding Lab exists for students who have the curiosity but not the network.

Students work one-on-one with a mentor to choose a question, learn the methods, and carry a project through to a conference presentation, with publication as a goal for projects that get there.

Who it's for

  • High school students in and around Irvine
  • Priority for first-generation college-bound students
  • Priority for students from groups underrepresented in STEM
  • No prior research or coding experience needed

Projects

Projects focus on computational biology, so students can do real research on a laptop, without access to a wet lab. These are examples. You and your mentor will choose a question that fits your interests.

Structure prediction

How does a mutation change a protein?

Compare predicted structures of a normal and disease-linked protein and explain what changes.

Molecular docking

Which molecules might bind a drug target?

Screen candidate compounds against a protein pocket and rank the most promising.

Molecular dynamics

How do flexible proteins move?

Simulate a protein over time and measure how its shape shifts and changes.

Bioinformatics

What can public data tell us?

Ask a question using open databases such as the Protein Data Bank and UniProt.

How it works

From first meeting to first presentation.

  1. Reach out

    Tell us a bit about yourself and what interests you.

  2. Find a question

    Work with your mentor to choose a project that fits your interests.

  3. Do the research

    Learn the tools, run the analysis, and interpret the results with regular check-ins.

  4. Share it

    Prepare a poster or talk for a conference, and a paper if the results support one.
